# Residency — permits, AIMA renewals, permanent residence, citizenship

> **Applies to:** foreign freelancers living in Portugal — third-country nationals on a residence permit (D7, D8, art. 89.º independent activity) and EU/EEA/Swiss citizens registered at the Câmara
> **Last verified:** 2026-10-02 — this area changed repeatedly in 2025–2026; re-check every date-bound fact before acting

AIMA (Agência para a Integração, Migrações e Asilo) replaced SEF in 2023. It issues and renews residence titles for non-EU nationals. EU citizens deal mainly with their Câmara Municipal. For a freelancer, the residency file and the tax file are linked: renewals check that you have no AT or SS debt, and the income you show AIMA is the income on your IRS. Keep them consistent.

This is not legal advice. Immigration rules here changed by decree several times in 2025–2026. Anything tagged *(unverified)* needs checking on aima.gov.pt or with a lawyer before you rely on it.

## Rules

### The permits a freelancer typically holds

| Title | For | Validity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AR for independent professional activity — art. 89.º n.º 1** Lei 23/2007 (after a residence visa) | trabalhador independente with Portuguese or foreign clients | **2 years** from issue, then renewable for **successive 3-year** periods | *(official source: AIMA page art. 89.º n.º 1)*. You can switch to employed work by asking AIMA to replace the title. |
| **D7 → AR** (own / passive income) | pensioners, rental and investment income | **2 years**, then renewable for successive **3-year** periods *(official source: art. 75.º n.º 1 Lei 23/2007 — the general rule for temporary permits)* | Means = **RMMG for the first adult (€920/month in 2026)**, +50% for each further adult, +30% per child *(official source: MNE Vistos "Meios de subsistência", citing DL 139/2025; Portaria 1563/2007)*. Art. 83.º n.º 1 c) Lei 23/2007 gives every residence-permit holder the right to carry on independent activity without special authorisation *(official source)*. Whether AIMA then renews a D7 on its passive-income basis or asks you to switch to an art. 89.º title is *(unverified — ask AIMA)*. |
| **D8 → AR** (remote work / "digital nomad") | remote work for employers or clients **outside Portugal** | **2 + 3 years** *(official source: art. 75.º n.º 1 Lei 23/2007)* | Visa test: average monthly income over the **last 3 months ≥ 4 × RMMG = €3 680/month (2026)** *(official source: MNE Vistos "Residência" checklist; RMMG 2026 = €920)*. The visa is defined as work done remotely for outside Portugal *(official source: MNE)*. Art. 83.º n.º 1 c) still lets the AR holder work independently. How AIMA treats mostly-Portuguese income at renewal is *(unverified)*. |
| **EU/EEA/Swiss citizen — Certificado de Registo** (CRUE) | stays over 3 months | up to 5 years | Issued by the **Câmara Municipal** where you live, on a sworn declaration of resources and health insurance where your home state requires it *(official source: AIMA; gov.pt; Lei 37/2006)*. Fee (Portaria 1334-D/2010 as amended by Portaria 13/2024): **over 25 — €18 in person, €15 online; under 25 — €15 in person, €12,50 online; under 1 — free** *(official source: AIMA Tabela de Taxas)*. |
| **EU citizen — Certificado de Residência Permanente** | after 5 years of legal residence | permanent | Requested at **AIMA through the Portal de Renovações** since 1 Jul 2026. It replaced the contact-form route. AIMA schedules an in-person visit only if it needs biometrics. Maximum decision time is 3 months *(official source: AIMA page and news, Jul 2026)* |

Lei 23/2007 (Lei de Estrangeiros) was amended by Lei 61/2025 (22 Oct) and again by **Lei 62/2026 (10 Sep 2026)**, which implements the EU Migration and Asylum Pact and narrows several in-country regularisation routes *(official source: consolidated text, PGDL; diariodarepublica.pt)*. This file doesn't cover family reunification, asylum or the art. 122.º changes.

### Renewal in 2026

- **Channel: Portal de Renovações**, `portal-renovacoes.aima.gov.pt` — renewals only, nothing else. *(official source: AIMA news, 21 Jul 2025)* It opens in batches by **expiry month**: in 2026 it reached titles expiring September–October 2026 *(official source: AIMA Facebook)*, then November–December 2026 (opened 3 Aug 2026 per DN Brasil, *unverified* — AIMA's Jul 2026 notice still listed coverage only up to titles expiring 31 Oct 2026). Golden Visa (ARI) renewals moved to the portal from 16 Feb 2026. *(official source: AIMA news)*
- **Titles that expired on or before 30 Jun 2025** are renewed by the **AIMA Estrutura de Missão** on a separate platform (services.aima.gov.pt). It invites holders by email in chronological order. *(official source: justica.gov.pt "Renovar Autorização de Residência")*
- **IRN (registry desks) stopped handling renewals on 1 Aug 2025.** *(official source: justica.gov.pt)*
- **When to apply:** as soon as the portal window for your expiry month opens. You can't apply before it opens. *(official source: AIMA portal notices)* The statute says to apply **up to 30 days before expiry** (art. 78.º n.º 1 Lei 23/2007). *(official source)*
- **Decision time:** the law gives AIMA **60 days** to decide a renewal (art. 82.º n.º 6 Lei 23/2007). *(official source)* In 2026, cards were reported arriving around 60 days after an online renewal. *(unverified — DN Brasil)*

### Expired titles: the end of the automatic extensions

- From 2020, a series of decree-laws extended the validity of expired residence documents. **The last one, DL 85-B/2025 (30 Jun 2025), extended them until 15 Oct 2025.** After that, a renewal is proven by AIMA's **comprovativo de pagamento do pedido de renovação, valid 180 days** from issue. *(official source: DL 85-B/2025)*
- After 15 Oct 2025, AIMA stated *(official source: AIMA "Esclarecimento: Direito de Residência", 17 Oct 2025)*:
  - an expired title is no longer valid unless renewal has started;
  - the **right of residence** lasts **6 months after the card's expiry date** (art. 63.º n.º 14 Decreto Regulamentar 84/2007);
  - for cards that expired by 30 Jun 2025, that right ran until **15 Apr 2026**.
- **As of 2026-10-02 no newer general extension was found.** *(unverified — check AIMA news before travelling on an expired card)*
- **Carry both:** the expired card **and** the renewal receipt (comprovativo). *(official source: AIMA, 17 Oct 2025)*

### What renewals usually ask for

*(official source: AIMA art. 89.º page and CPLP renewal FAQ, unless tagged; the exact list depends on the title)*

- **Valid passport** and the current residence card.
- **Address:** a sworn declaration of residence explaining how you live there. If you own the home, a certidão de registo predial or its access code. If renting, a **declaration from the landlord** stating the legal basis of your use of the home. *(unverified for third-country renewals: an atestado de residência from the Junta — AIMA lists it as address proof only on its EU-citizen pages)*
- **Means of subsistence:** IRS declaration (Modelo 3 + nota de liquidação), contracts or service agreements, or other proof of stable, regular income.
- **No debt to AT and SS:** AIMA checks the databases. The CPLP FAQ says no declaração negativa is needed. Bring the AT não-dívida certidão and the SS declaração de situação contributiva anyway — see [`certidoes.md`](certidoes.md). *(bringing them: unverified practice)*
- **Criminal record:** the application includes your **authorisation for AIMA to check the Portuguese registo criminal**. A foreign record from your nationality country is typical for first grants, and appears in some renewal lists (CPLP). *(official source: AIMA CPLP FAQ)*
- **For art. 89.º:** proof the activity continues — open activity at AT, recent recibos or contracts, a professional-body declaration where the profession is regulated. *(partly official: AIMA art. 89.º page; the recibos part unverified)*
- **Fees:** paid by DUC when submitting. The payment receipt is your interim proof.

### Permanent residence (non-EU)

- **Autorização de residência permanente** (art. 80.º Lei 23/2007) after holding a temporary permit for **5 years**, with means of subsistence, accommodation, **basic Portuguese** and no prison sentence(s) over 1 year in the last 5 years. AIMA also asks for proof that tax and SS obligations are met. Accepted language proofs include a Portuguese school certificate, an IEFP or recognised basic-Portuguese course, a CAPLE test, or an **A2** Português Língua de Acolhimento certificate. *(official source: art. 80.º Lei 23/2007; AIMA art. 80.º page)*
- **Where to apply:** by appointment, **in person at any Loja AIMA** *(official source: AIMA art. 80.º page)*. The 1 Jul 2026 move to the **Portal de Renovações** covers only permanent documents for **EU citizens and their family members** *(official source: AIMA news, Jul 2026)*.
- **Absences:** AIMA *may* cancel a permit after an absence without good reason of **6 consecutive or 8 interpolated months** in the validity of a temporary permit, or **24 consecutive months / 30 interpolated months in 3 years** for a permanent one. Longer absences must be justified to AIMA before leaving. A permit isn't cancelled if you prove you worked professionally abroad during the absence. *(official source: art. 85.º n.ºs 2–4 Lei 23/2007, current consolidated text)* Nomads should log travel days.

### Citizenship by naturalisation — the 2026 change

- **Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, of 18 May** (rectified by Declaração de Retificação 17/2026) amended the Lei da Nacionalidade (Lei 37/81). **In force since 19 May 2026.** *(official source: justica.gov.pt)*
- **Minimum legal residence for naturalisation:** **7 years** for nationals of EU member states and Portuguese-speaking (CPLP) countries; **10 years** for everyone else. It used to be 5 for all. *(official source: justica.gov.pt)*
- **New requirements** (art. 6.º n.º 1) *(official source: justica.gov.pt)*:
  - sufficient knowledge of Portuguese culture, history and national symbols;
  - knowledge of citizens' rights and duties and the political organisation;
  - a solemn declaration of adherence to the rule of law;
  - ability to support yourself;
  - no prison sentence over 3 years for listed serious crimes;
  - no UN/EU sanctions.
- **Applications filed before 19 May 2026 follow the old law.** *(official source: justica.gov.pt)*
- **When the clock starts:** legal residence means time spent in Portugal with regular status under any title, visa or authorisation of the Lei de Estrangeiros (art. 15.º n.º 1 Lei 37/81). The 2024 rule that counted from the date of the residence application (old n.º 4) is **revoked**. Separate periods add up only if they all fall within **9 years** (CPLP/EU nationals) or **12 years** (others) (art. 15.º n.º 3). *(official source: Lei 37/81 as amended by LO 1/2026, consolidated text)* So the count runs from when you first held a qualifying visa or permit, not from the application date *(inference from art. 15.º)*. Whether the visa period before the first card counts is *(unverified — check with the IRN)*.
- The language-and-culture test format depends on the amended Regulamento da Nacionalidade. The Government had 90 days from 19 May 2026 to adopt it. *(official source for the 90 days)* Whether it has been adopted is *(unverified — Público reported on 30 Aug 2026 that it was still missing; no adoption found as of 2 Oct 2026)*.
- History: the first 2025 version was sent back after a Constitutional Court ruling. Parliament approved a new text on 1 Apr 2026, and it was promulgated on 3 May 2026. *(unverified — secondary press)*

## How to do it

1. **Know your dates.** Record in `profile.md`: the permit type and article, the expiry date, and the issue date of your **first** card. No card numbers. That last one may now drive the citizenship count.
2. **~4 months before expiry:** watch AIMA's news page for your expiry month's portal window. Clean up AT and SS (no debts, declarations filed) — see [`social-security.md`](social-security.md) and [`at-communication.md`](at-communication.md).
3. **When your window opens:** portal-renovacoes.aima.gov.pt → authenticate → upload documents → pay the DUC → **save the comprovativo** (PDF) immediately.
4. **While waiting:** carry the expired card plus the comprovativo. Note the comprovativo's own validity (180 days under DL 85-B/2025 for this receipt type) and diarise it.
5. **Change of address, NIF or name:** use AIMA's Formulário de Contacto → "Alteração de Dados". The renewals portal doesn't process them. *(official source: AIMA news, 21 Jul 2025)*
6. **EU citizens:** CRUE at the Câmara after 3 months; permanent card at AIMA after 5 years.

## Gotchas

- **The renewals portal only renews.** Data changes, permit-type changes and permanent residence (outside the cases AIMA has moved there) go through the contact form or an appointment.
- **SS and AT debts surface at renewal.** A forgotten SS declaração trimestral or an unpaid coima can stall it. Pull both certidões a month before your window.
- **Income shown to AIMA must match your IRS.** D7/D8 means tests are read against declared income. A freelancer who under-declared in Portugal has a renewal problem as well as a tax one.
- **Expired card + comprovativo works inside Portugal.** Airlines and other Schengen border posts may not accept it. *(unverified — widely reported)* Plan travel around the card, not the receipt.
- **Citizenship timelines quoted before 19 May 2026 are obsolete** for new applications: 7 or 10 years now, not 5.
- **Don't confuse the two "registos":** the EU citizen's Certificado de Registo comes from the Câmara. The registo criminal comes from Justiça.
